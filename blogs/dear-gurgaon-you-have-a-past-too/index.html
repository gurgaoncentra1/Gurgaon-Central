Dear Gurgaon, you have a past too!

By Rithvika

Dear Gurgaon,

Coming from a Maharashtrian family, all I had ever heard about were Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, Shri Tulja Bhavani Temple, Ganesh Chaturthi, and the Mahabharat, which has always fascinated me. Yet, despite growing up in Gurgaon, I never truly realised that the city's name was rooted in the story of Guru Dronacharya. Yes, the great teacher himself!

According to legend, a piece of land was gifted to Guru Dronacharya by the eldest of the Pandavas as gurudakshina, an offering of gratitude to their teacher for imparting the knowledge of warfare and the lessons of life to both the Pandavas and the Kauravas. Since Guru Dronacharya lived here, the place came to be known as Guru Ka Gaon, which eventually evolved into Gurugram and, later, Gurgaon.

Although the city's official name is now Gurugram, many people still affectionately call it Gurgaon, much like how Bangaloreans continue to use the name Bangalore instead of Bengaluru.

Amidst the fancy skyscrapers, bustling streets, and corporate offices, Gurgaon is home to several historic sites associated with the legends of the Mahabharat. Take Khandsa village in Sector 37, for instance, which locals believe to be the place where Ekalavya cut off his thumb and offered it to Dronacharya as gurudakshina. Then there's the Dronacharya Temple, one of the rare temples in India dedicated to the great teacher himself, and Bhima Kund, a pond believed to be associated with Guru Dronacharya. I guess it was a time when stalking culture was allowed!

And finally, the most famous of them all: Sheetla Mata Temple, which attracts thousands of devotees every year. The temple is associated with Kripi, also known as Kriparani or Kripa in some local retellings, the wife of Guru Dronacharya. According to local traditions, she devoted her life to caring for children suffering from smallpox. After her death, a temple was established in her memory, and to this day, Sheetla Mata remains one of the most revered goddesses in the region.

Isn't that beautiful?

A city known for its towering skyscrapers, hustle culture, chai tapris, and breathtaking skies has a past that stretches far beyond its modern identity. Beneath all the urban chaos lies a world of legends, faith, and history that connects Gurgaon to one of India's greatest epics.

Perhaps that's what makes this city so special. It's not just a place where people come to build careers, chase dreams, or survive the daily traffic. It's also a place where stories from thousands of years ago continue to live on through temples, traditions, and the beliefs of its people.

So, the next time you pass through Gurgaon remember that it's a city where the ancient and the modern coexist, where mythology meets everyday life, and where every corner might have a story waiting to be discovered.
