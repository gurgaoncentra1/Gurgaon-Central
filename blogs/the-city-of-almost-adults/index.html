The City of Almost Adults

By Garima

There is something about growing up in Gurgaon that makes you feel older than you are.

Maybe it is the buildings.

When I was younger, I thought the glass towers were just part of the scenery. Now, every time I drive past them, I wonder how many people inside are answering emails, rushing to meetings, or pretending to understand what their boss just said.

At seventeen, I already knew people who were talking about internships, college applications, careers and five-year plans.

Five-year plans.

I could barely decide what I wanted for dinner.

Gurgaon has a strange way of making adulthood look very close. You see people in formal clothes getting out of cabs at 9 in the morning. You see cafés filled with people working on laptops. You hear conversations about salaries, promotions, investments and “where do you see yourself in five years?”

And somewhere between school and college, you start asking yourself the same questions. What are you going to do? Where are you going to study? What job do you want? How much do you want to earn? What are you going to become?

As a teenager, I thought growing up meant finally having all the answers.

But Gurgaon has taught me that most adults are probably figuring it out too.

I remember sitting in cafés with my friends, pretending to have serious conversations about our futures. One person wanted to study abroad. Someone else wanted to start a business. Someone had already decided which company they wanted to work for.

And I would nod along, as if I had everything figured out. I didn't. I still don't. But maybe that's okay.

Maybe that's what Gurgaon has really taught me about growing up.

You can spend years watching a city change around you without realising that you're changing with it. The empty plots become buildings. The familiar roads get wider. New cafés replace old ones. Your friends leave for college, come back with new stories, and suddenly everyone is asking what comes next.

And somewhere in between all of that, you realise you're not the same person who used to look out of the car window at those glass towers and wonder what happened inside them.

One day, you might be inside one of them. Maybe you'll have the laptop, the formal clothes, the meetings and the five-year plan.

Or maybe you'll change your mind completely.

I think I'm finally okay with either. Because if Gurgaon has taught me anything, it's that nothing here stays the same for very long.

Not the skyline.

Not the city.

And definitely not us.

So maybe I don't need to know exactly where I'm going yet. For now, I'm just growing up with the city that taught me how to keep moving, even when I haven't figured out where I'm headed.
