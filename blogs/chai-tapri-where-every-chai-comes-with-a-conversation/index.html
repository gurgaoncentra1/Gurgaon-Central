Chai Tapri: Where Every Chai Comes with a Conversation

By Rithvika

In a city known for its bougie cafés, towering skyscrapers, corporate life, and hustle culture, I find that chai tapris have the best conversations.

Imagine walking into a random chai tapri at six in the morning. Before your chai even reaches you, you would have heard six different political opinions, starting with the Mosquito Janata Party, and seven different recommendations on how to fix the country's economy. By the time you finish your chai, you've been pulled into a conversation, and by the end of it, you either feel educated or face an existential crisis.

Now imagine the evening. You've just come down from your demanding corporate job, sometime around four or later, and all you want is a cup of chai, perhaps a biscuit, and a moment to think about your life choices. But before you can even finish your chai, you're pulled into another conversation. Someone is ranting about how ridiculous their boss is, someone else is explaining how they had to skip lunch to finish a pile of work the size of a skyscraper in Sector 37, and another person is talking about quitting their job to build a startup, which everyone knows isn't happening anytime soon.

By the time I get into the lift and head back to my demanding job, I have even more life choices to ponder.

But somewhere between all the existential crises and endless pondering, it is the beauty of human connection that keeps me going.

Chai tapris are places where people aren't scrolling through Instagram, Facebook, or the latest obsession, Substack. Instead, they're there for fifteen minutes of human interaction, a little break from the world, and perhaps a cup of chai. So they start talking about random things: politics, religion, work, life, and everything in between. Somehow, people join in, adding their opinions and stories without turning every conversation into an argument or a formal debate.

Slowly, these conversations become an accepted part of your day. You find yourself returning to the same tapri, again and again. The people who once seemed like complete strangers start becoming familiar faces. You recognise them, exchange a few words, hear about their lives, and share a little of your own.

Before you know it, these people are no longer random strangers. They have become a small but meaningful part of your everyday story.

And honestly, isn't that beautiful?
