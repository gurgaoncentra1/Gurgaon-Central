Finding Home Away From Home

By Dhiya

At 18, moving to Gurgaon to call it home after living all my life in Chennai was nothing less than a scary experience. As I was boarding my flight to Delhi so many thoughts ran through my head, “will I be able to make friends in a new city?”, “can I handle things without my family?”, “will I be treated differently by people because I am from the south”. But when I entered Gurgaon for the first time, I was amazed by the tall beautiful buildings that decorated the roads. As an aspiring entrepreneur and business student I was completely in awe looking at all the companies I had only dreamed of, ranging from BMW to Deloitte, all just right in front of my eyes as I travelled along the city to my hostel.

For someone obsessed with cars, Gurgaon is like a dreamland, with expensive supercars strutting down the road as if it were a normal affair. They say that you are the product of the people you surround yourself with, and Gurgaon is filled with great minds and successful individuals. Slowly my mindset started shifting in order to think the way they do and reach the levels that they did. Once my classes started, I realised that there were a lot of local students and I was beyond nervous to see how they would treat me. But contrary to my assumptions, the people were as amazing as the city, very sweet and friendly. More than half of my closest friends are natives to the place and I am really glad that I found them.

But one thing that Gurgaon can never replace in my heart is the food. Don’t get me wrong, Gurgaon has amazing food spots, whether you go to Galleria or 32nd Avenue, it not only gives you delicious meals but also a great ambience to enjoy the meal in. But my mother’s crispy dosa will always hold a special place in my heart and nothing can ever compare to it. Something that made me fall deeper in love with the city was it’s weather. Having lived in the South, I had only known what summers were but Gurgaon showed me what a real winter feels like. And let me tell you it is definitely not like putting only the AC, with the fan turned off while sleeping during the Chennai winters. It took me by shock but as a person who loves eating ice cream when it is freezing, I loved it.

Now, after spending 3 years in Gurgaon I can finally say that my heart belongs to the city. It has moulded me into the individual I am today, helping me face the real world in the big city. All my fears of people, goals, and life in general were removed and the city welcomed me with open hands. Gurgaon has my heart and it can never be forgotten.
